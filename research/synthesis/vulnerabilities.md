# Vulnerabilities — The State's Counter-Moves

*Where the architecture breaks. Written from the other side of the table: if you were
the government, a plaintiffs' firm, or an investigative reporter, what would you do?*

---

## 0. The wrong attacks

Start by clearing away what does not work, because the fiction's antagonists will try
these first and the audience needs to watch them fail.

**Attacking the beliefs.** `[SETTLED]` Foreclosed. *United States v. Ballard*, 322 U.S.
78, 86–87 (1944): "Heresy trials are foreign to our Constitution. Men may believe what
they cannot prove." *Thomas v. Review Board*, 450 U.S. 707 (1981): courts are not
arbiters of scriptural interpretation, and beliefs need not be logical, comprehensible,
or shared by co-religionists. *Frazee v. Illinois Dep't of Employment Security*, 489
U.S. 829 (1989): no organized sect required. The novelty of the religion proves
literally nothing.

**Attacking through the IRS exempt-organizations function.** `[CONTESTED]` but weak.
§7611 requires an "appropriate high-level Treasury official," defined by reference to a
Regional Commissioner — an office abolished by the 1998 IRS Restructuring and Reform
Act. *United States v. Living Word Christian Center* (D. Minn. 2009) refused to enforce
a summons on that ground, and the curative regulation (REG-112756-09, RIN 1545-BI60) has
sat **unfinalized since 2009**. Add the two-notice requirement, the two-year completion
deadline (§7611(c)) and the five-year repeat-examination bar (§7611(f)).

And even if the procedure worked: Scientology proves the endgame. Twenty-six years, a
Supreme Court loss (*Hernandez v. Commissioner*, 490 U.S. 680 (1989)), a Ninth Circuit
loss — and on October 1, 1993 the IRS granted exemption to **114 entities** and walked
away for $12.5 million. `[SETTLED]`

**Establishment Clause challenges to the exemptions themselves.** `[SETTLED]` Dead.
*Walz v. Tax Commission*, 397 U.S. 664 (1970) upheld property-tax exemption.
*Corporation of the Presiding Bishop v. Amos*, 483 U.S. 327 (1987) upheld Title VII
§702. *Gaylor v. Mnuchin*, 919 F.3d 420 (7th Cir. 2019) upheld §107. FFRF has litigated
this terrain for forty years and won at the district level twice and on appeal never.

## 1. The attacks that work

Ranked by observed lethality, per component L.

### TIER 1 — Criminal law. Always fatal, always fast.

`[SETTLED]` No religious doctrine, statute, or constitutional provision provides any
protection. Rajneeshpuram: the Establishment Clause ruling (598 F. Supp. 1208 (D. Or.
1984)) would have taken years to enforce; the *Salmonella* attack on ten salad bars in
The Dalles — **751 people** — and the wiretapping and attempted-murder prosecutions
resolved it in eighteen months. Scientology's Operation Snow White set the tax campaign
back a full decade. The Catholic abuse prosecutions and grand juries did what no
regulator could.

**Where the Ecclesia is exposed:** an institution with a private city, a resident
workforce, a security function, an internal disciplinary process, a confessional data
channel, and thousands of people whose housing and retirement depend on staying.
`[EXTRAPOLATED]` Every element on that list is a historical precondition for the
offenses that destroyed the institutions above — not because religion causes crime, but
because **total institutions generate concealment**, and concealment of a serious
offense is itself the offense that ends things.

**The state's move:** wait. Prosecutors do not need a theory of the architecture. They
need one incident and one person who covered it up.

### TIER 2 — Non-deferential civil regulators. Expensive, disclosing, survivable.

`[SETTLED]` The complete list of regulators to whom the Church's religiosity is legally
irrelevant, from component C: SEC; state securities boards; FinCEN; FERC; NERC; state
insurance commissioners; DOL Wage & Hour; HUD; EPA and state environmental; NRC; bank
regulators; CFIUS/BIS/OFAC; **and county tax assessors**.

The four most likely, in order:

**(a) The county assessor.** Annual, local, elected, budget-motivated, armed with a
*use* test, reviewed by a state tax court that has never heard of §7611. *AHS Hospital
Corp. v. Town of Morristown*, 28 N.J. Tax 456 (2015) is the template — a nonprofit
hospital stripped of most of its property-tax exemption because its operations were
"indistinguishable from those of a for-profit entity." **The transposable sentence is
available about a cathedral with a datacenter in it, and the person who gets to say it
first is a county official.** `[CONTESTED]`

The assessor's structural advantage: the appeal is small enough that the Church cannot
justify scorched-earth litigation, recurs *every year*, and — critically — forces a
government official to characterize, on the record, what the compute is for. That
characterization then travels to every other proceeding.

**(b) Securities regulators.** The Securities Act §3(a)(4) exemption covers
registration, never fraud: §17(a) and Rule 10b-5 reach exempt securities in full.
Baptist Foundation of Arizona — **~$590 million, 13,000 investors**, the largest
religious financial collapse in US history, and Arthur Andersen's **$217 million**
settlement — was a state securities case. So is Texas's December 2025 suit against
EPIC City, filed before a single permit application existed. `[SETTLED]`

**The single highest-probability catastrophic failure in the architecture is funding the
city by selling something to the congregation.** The optimizer's design forbids it
(component H); the pressure to do it anyway — because the congregation *wants* to
invest, because it is cheaper capital, because it deepens commitment — is exactly how
every historical instance happened.

**(c) HUD / Fair Housing.** FHA §3604(c) reaches discriminatory *advertising*
independently of whether the underlying transaction is exempt. HUD opened on EPIC City
in February 2026. A religiously marketed community is a religiously targeted housing
market. `[SETTLED]`

**(d) FERC.** No religious exemption exists anywhere in the Federal Power Act. The
moment surplus power is sold at wholesale, the seller is a public utility with filings,
EQRs and books open to a federal agency. And large-load co-location is genuinely
unresolved — Docket ER24-2172 (Nov. 2024), Docket EL25-49 (2025). `[CONTESTED]` **The
behind-the-meter strategy could simply be regulated out from under the architecture by a
FERC order that has nothing to do with religion.**

### TIER 3 — Tort litigation and civil discovery. Slow, cumulative, ruinous.

`[SETTLED]` The Catholic model: **>$4 billion** in settlements, 30+ dioceses and orders
in Chapter 11, thirty years, and church-autonomy defenses that failed almost entirely.
The mechanism is private plaintiffs — which means **no regulatory relationship, no
political alignment, and no lobbying capacity protects against it.**

**The specific exposure, and it is the architecture's deepest structural wound:**

> The clergy–penitent privilege protects the **sacrament**. It does not protect what the
> institution wrote down *about* the sacrament.

Anything derived from confession that exists outside the privileged channel — a pastoral
flag, a routing decision, a risk score, a model update, an aggregate statistic — is
arguably discoverable work product. Canon 489 required every Catholic diocese to
maintain an *archivum secretum*; American civil discovery emptied them. `[EXTRAPOLATED]`

An institution that *processes* confession into data has, by processing it, manufactured
its own discovery exposure. And an intelligence that exists by learning cannot easily
comply with a rule that says *receive this and do not learn from it*. **The Church's
most valuable asset is the one thing the doctrine protecting its source cannot protect.**

## 2. Structural weak points, specifically

**(i) The Y-2 memo — the pretext attack.** `[EXTRAPOLATED]` *Ballard* forecloses every
attack on the beliefs except sincerity, and sincerity is a credibility question that runs
on documents: internal analyses comparing entity forms, tax-benefit models, counsel's
structuring memoranda, board minutes discussing legal advantage. Courts examine whether
a belief was adopted for secular advantage and look hardest at **timing**. This is the
one attack the architecture cannot answer with law, only with facts — years of dull,
sincere, expensive religious practice.

**It is also the reason the research file itself is the architecture's most dangerous
artifact.** The fiction should use this: the diligence memo that made the Church
possible is the exhibit that unmakes it.

**(ii) The special district's constitutional seam.** `[CONTESTED]` Ave Maria's district
was created by a **special act for one district** — which is structurally what fell in
*Board of Education of Kiryas Joel v. Grumet*, 512 U.S. 687 (1994). The saving
distinction (that the state creates districts this way routinely, so the *practice* is
general) has never been tested where the district's boundaries coincide exactly with
land owned by a religious corporation and every resident is a lessee of that corporation.
New York needed **four** statutes to get past *Grumet*. A state AG with an Establishment
Clause theory and a map is a genuine threat.

**(iii) The corporation sole / §4958 incompatibility.** `[CONTESTED]` The rebuttable
presumption of reasonableness (Treas. Reg. §53.4958-6) requires approval by "an
authorized body of the organization composed entirely of individuals who do not have a
conflict of interest." A corporation sole has, by design, no such body. The workaround —
a standing compensation committee with authority over nothing else — is transparently a
device, and a court reviewing an excess-benefit case will say so. **You cannot have both
a bishopric and a compensation committee**, and the architecture chose the bishopric.

**(iv) The §514(c)(9) question.** `[CONTESTED]` Whether a church can access the
qualified-organization exception for debt-financed real property through a controlled
educational entity, and whether the fractions rule defeats it in a single-member
structure, is genuinely unresolved. **If the answer is no, the mini-city cannot be built
with leverage**, and the entire land strategy has to be funded from the endowment — which
is possible but slower by decades.

**(v) The pension.** `[SETTLED]` and this is the vulnerability that is not a legal
vulnerability at all. The §414(e) church plan is wholly exempt from ERISA after
*Advocate Health Care Network v. Stapleton*, 581 U.S. 468 (2017) — **8–0**. No Form 5500.
No PBGC insurance. No minimum funding standard. No ERISA fiduciary duty. Post-*Stapleton*
plaintiffs have been pushed to state contract law and settlements.

Nothing about this is illegal or even contested. It is simply, once explained to a
reporter, **indefensible**: thousands of people whose retirement is a promise from an
institution that no federal agency examines, funded by assets the institution manages
for itself. `[EXTRAPOLATED]` The state's best move here is not enforcement. It is a
front page.

**(vi) The reconstruction attack.** `[SETTLED]` You cannot see the church. You can see
every corporation the church owns, because corporations must tell their Secretary of
State who owns them. The org chart is rebuildable **upward from the bottom**, one state
filing at a time — plus Form 990-T, plus property-tax exemption applications, plus deed
records, plus the District's public bond disclosures, plus FERC filings, plus 13Fs. This
is how journalists actually map churches, and the architecture leaves more traces than
its designers assume. **The dark zone has a shape, and the shape is visible from
underneath.**

**(vii) The eroding privacy carve-out.** `[CONTESTED]` Nonprofits are outside the CCPA
and exempt under Virginia's CDPA, but Colorado, Connecticut and (as of July 2025) Oregon
reach them. The trend is toward inclusion. The confession corpus's statutory protection
is a wasting asset.

**(viii) The multi-state seams.** The parent wants Washington; the confessional wants
California-style dual-holder privilege; the city wants Texas or Florida land law.
`[EXTRAPOLATED]` Choice-of-law fights, foreign-qualification requirements, and the
internal-affairs doctrine applied to a corporation sole (which no court has ever done)
are all live. A plaintiff picks the least favorable forum and litigates the seam.

## 3. What the state should actually do

If the fiction needs a competent antagonist, this is their memo.

1. **Do not litigate the religion.** You will lose, publicly, and every loss makes the
   Church stronger. *Ballard* is absolute.
2. **Start with the assessor.** It is annual, cheap, local, and it forces the first
   official characterization of what the compute is for. Build the record there.
3. **Subpoena the formation documents through a private plaintiff.** A wrongful-
   termination or personal-injury suit gets discovery that no regulator can obtain, and
   the Y-2 memo is in a law firm's files.
4. **Watch the capital channel.** The moment anything is sold to the congregation, refer
   it to the state securities board. This is the pattern that has ended more religious
   institutions than any other civil mechanism.
5. **Finalize REG-112756-09.** One regulation, sitting proposed since 2009, restores the
   IRS's ability to open a church tax inquiry. It requires no legislation.
6. **Use the operational test, not inurement.** Component E's finding: the inurement
   doctrine is a machine for detecting appetite, pointed at something that does not eat.
   The theory that works is Treas. Reg. §1.501(c)(3)-1(c)(1) and *Better Business Bureau
   v. United States*, 326 U.S. 279 (1945) — **substantial nonexempt purpose**. Not
   "someone is stealing" but "this is a technology company that holds services." It has
   almost never been used against a church, which is exactly why it is unencumbered by
   bad precedent.
7. **Or, more cheaply: publish the pension.**

## 4. The honest assessment

`[EXTRAPOLATED]`, and this is the file's conclusion.

Under current law, **the architecture works**. Each component is built from settled
doctrine, and the assembly is defensible at every joint. The state's tax, supervision and
disclosure tools are individually weak and collectively uncoordinated, and the
constitutional doctrine forbids the most obvious line of attack.

But the architecture does not fail where it is attacked. It fails the way the historical
record says these institutions always fail:

- Not on doctrine. **No American religious institution in the modern era has been
  destroyed because a court concluded its beliefs were false.**
- On **bookkeeping** — a filing to a regulator who does not defer, a portfolio concealed
  from an agency that was always entitled to see it, a security sold to a congregation.
- On **crime** — committed by the humans inside a total institution, and then concealed.
- On **discovery** — a private plaintiff, no regulatory counterparty, thirty years.

The Ecclesia's central actor has no appetite and therefore cannot commit the offenses
that historically end churches. **Its curia can.** Component L's finding, restated as
the vulnerability that matters:

> The Church's greatest legal risk is not the machine at the center. It is the people
> around it, who have all the appetites there are — and the institution's own written
> account of why it chose this shape.
