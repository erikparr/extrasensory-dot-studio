# C. Opacity & Audit Shielding

**Question:** How dark can the entity legally be, and which regulators do not care?

---

## 1. The Form 990 exemption — the informational dark zone

**IRC §6033(a)(3)(A)(i)** exempts from the annual information return requirement
"churches, their integrated auxiliaries, and conventions or associations of churches"
`[SETTLED]`. Treas. Reg. §1.6033-2(g)(1)(i) implements it. There is also a
discretionary exemption in §6033(a)(3)(B) and Rev. Proc. 96-10 for certain
church-affiliated organizations.

What Form 990 would otherwise disclose, and therefore what is *not* disclosed:

| Schedule | Content withheld |
|---|---|
| Core form Part VIII–X | Total revenue, expenses, net assets, investments |
| Part VII / Schedule J | Officer, director, trustee and key employee compensation |
| Schedule B | Substantial contributors (IRS copy) |
| Schedule D | Donor-advised funds, endowments, art and historic treasures, other assets |
| Schedule F | Foreign activities and grantmaking by region |
| Schedule I | Domestic grants |
| Schedule K | Tax-exempt bond issues |
| Schedule L | Transactions with interested persons |
| Schedule R | Related organizations and unrelated partnerships — **the org chart** |

Schedule R is the one that matters most here. For every other large exempt
organization in America, the topology described in component A would be a public
document. For a church, it does not exist.

`[SETTLED]` A church **must** still file **Form 990-T** if it has $1,000+ of gross
unrelated business income — and 990-T is publicly disclosable for §501(c)(3)
organizations under §6104(d)(1)(A)(ii) (added by the 2006 Pension Protection Act).
This is the leak in the dark zone: **the only routinely public federal document about
a church's finances is the tax return covering the part of its business the government
considers non-religious.** The 990-T is a shadow-portrait, drawn entirely in the
negative space of the church's own claims about itself.

Churches also file: Forms W-2/941 (payroll), 1099s, Form 5578 (racially
nondiscriminatory policy for church schools), and state property-tax exemption
applications (component J) — which are often the richest publicly available source of
information about church real assets.

## 2. §7611: what it takes to even look

**IRC §7611**, the Church Audit Procedures Act of 1984, is the procedural moat
`[SETTLED]`.

The IRS may not begin a **church tax inquiry** unless:

1. An **"appropriate high-level Treasury official"** reasonably believes, **on the
   basis of facts and circumstances recorded in writing**, that the organization may
   not qualify for exemption or may be carrying on unrelated business
   (§7611(a)(2)); and
2. The IRS gives **written notice** to the church of the beginning of the inquiry,
   containing an explanation of the concerns, the general subject matter, and the
   church's rights (§7611(a)(3)).

Escalation to a **church tax examination** (examining books or religious activities)
requires a second written notice at least 15 days in advance, an offer of a conference
with the church, and — for examination of religious activities — additional constraints
(§7611(b)). Regional counsel approval is required for certain examinations.

**Completion deadlines** (§7611(c)): inquiries and examinations must be completed
within **2 years** of the examination notice date, with limited suspensions.

**Limits on repeat examinations** (§7611(f)): if a prior inquiry or examination did not
result in revocation, notice of deficiency, or a request for a significant change in
operations, the IRS generally may not begin another inquiry within **5 years**.

### The structural defect the IRS has never fixed

`[CONTESTED]` — and this is the single best piece of legal texture in the entire
research file.

§7611(h)(7) defines "appropriate high-level Treasury official" as the Secretary or a
delegate "whose rank is no lower than that of a principal Internal Revenue officer for
an internal revenue region." When §7611 was enacted in 1984, that meant a **Regional
Commissioner**, reporting directly to the Commissioner of Internal Revenue.

**In 1998, the IRS Restructuring and Reform Act abolished the regional structure.**
There are no Regional Commissioners. The statutory office referenced by the church-audit
threshold **no longer exists.**

In *United States v. Living Word Christian Center*, No. 08-mc-37 (D. Minn. 2009), the
court held that the **Director, Exempt Organizations (Examinations)** was *not* a
sufficiently high-level Treasury official under §7611, and refused to enforce the IRS
summons `[SETTLED]` at the district-court level. The IRS proposed regulations
(REG-112756-09) to designate the Director, Exempt Organizations as the appropriate
official. **Those regulations have sat unfinalized since 2009**, reappearing on
successive Priority Guidance Plans (RIN 1545-BI60) without ever being promulgated
`[CONTESTED]` — verified as still pending on the 2025 unified agenda.

The consequence, stated plainly: **for roughly the last fifteen years, the Internal
Revenue Service has been in a state of acknowledged procedural uncertainty about
whether anyone at the agency is legally authorized to open a church tax inquiry.**
Church examinations have accordingly been rare — the IRS conducted a handful annually
even before *Living Word*, and effectively paused thereafter.

`[EXTRAPOLATED]` An optimizer reading this does not conclude "we will never be
audited." It concludes something more useful: **the audit risk is not a function of the
Church's conduct. It is a function of whether a specific regulation gets finalized.**
That converts an operational risk into a *legislative-calendar* risk, which is a risk an
entity with unbounded patience and lobbying capacity manages differently. It also
supplies a clock for the fiction: the day REG-112756-09 is finalized is a plot point.

## 3. Where opacity failed: SEC v. Ensign Peak Advisers

**In the Matter of Ensign Peak Advisers, Inc. and The Church of Jesus Christ of
Latter-day Saints**, SEC Admin. Proc. (Feb. 21, 2023) `[SETTLED]`.

Facts per the SEC order: Ensign Peak, the LDS Church's investment manager, held equity
positions requiring quarterly **Form 13F** filings under Exchange Act §13(f) and Rule
13f-1. From **1997 to 2019**, rather than file in its own name, Ensign Peak created
**thirteen shell LLCs** in locations across the United States and filed Forms 13F in
their names, misstating ownership and control. Business managers with no investment
authority signed the filings. The stated motive in the order: concern that disclosure
of the portfolio's size — **$32 billion by 2018** — would provoke negative reaction
among church members.

Penalties: **$4 million** (Ensign Peak) and **$1 million** (the Church) `[SETTLED]`.

The lesson is not "don't lie to the SEC." It is jurisdictional:

> **Tax law defers to churches. Securities law does not know what a church is.**

Section 13(f) applies to "institutional investment managers" exercising investment
discretion over $100 million+ in §13(f) securities. The definition contains no
religious carve-out, no sincerity inquiry, no First Amendment accommodation. The
Church's religiosity was legally irrelevant to whether it had to file. The entire
elaborate deference apparatus — §6033, §7611, church autonomy, AG exemptions — **stops
at the boundary of the securities laws** and does not resume.

## 5. The synthesis rule

`[EXTRAPOLATED]` from the pattern across components C and L:

> Religious deference in American law is **subject-matter specific, not
> entity-specific.** There is no such thing as a generally-deferred-to entity. There
> are only regimes that have written a religious exception into their own text, and
> regimes that have not. The exceptions cluster in **tax, employment-discrimination,
> land use, and charitable supervision.** They are absent from **securities, banking,
> energy, antitrust, export control, environmental, and criminal law.**

An optimizer therefore designs the entity so that as much activity as possible occurs
inside the four deferential regimes, and so that activity which must cross into a
non-deferential regime does so **through a taxable subsidiary that is fully, boringly
compliant** — a compliance lightning rod that gives the non-deferential regulator a
legible, cooperative counterparty and no reason to reach up the chain.

The Ensign Peak failure was precisely the opposite move: the Church tried to extend
*tax-style* opacity into a *securities* regime. It worked for twenty-two years and then
cost $5 million and the entire portfolio's confidentiality at once.

## (a) The optimizer's likely choice

Accept total transparency at the taxable-subsidiary layer; maintain total silence at the
church layer; **never let the two be the same entity.** File 13Fs. File 990-Ts, and
manage what appears on them, since the 990-T is the only routine public window.
Register the investment manager as an investment adviser if the facts require it, and
staff it with people whose names on filings are true. Treat the §7611 shield as a
durable but *finalizable* advantage — the regulation could be promulgated in any given
year — and never build a plan whose survival depends on the IRS remaining unable to
open an inquiry.

Above all: **do not conceal.** The dark zone is created by statutes that *do not ask*.
Every catastrophic religious-institution failure in this research file involved
affirmative misstatement to a regulator that did ask.

## (c) Open questions

1. If REG-112756-09 is finalized, does the §7611 shield materially weaken, or does the
   two-notice / two-year / five-year structure still make examinations impractical?
   `[CONTESTED]`
2. Does an integrated auxiliary that operates a datacenter remain "internally
   supported" if it sells any capacity? (Component D.)
3. Is there any obligation to disclose the *existence* of the corporation sole's
   subsidiaries anywhere? State annual reports for the taxable subsidiaries name their
   shareholder — so the org chart is reconstructable **upward from the bottom**, one
   Secretary of State filing at a time. This is how journalists actually map churches.
4. Would an ASI-operated investment process trigger Investment Advisers Act
   registration for the entity itself? `[EXTRAPOLATED]` — the Act reaches persons who
   advise *others*; a church managing only its own money is generally outside it, which
   is why Ensign Peak's exposure was 13(f), not registration.

## (d) Narrative-usable details

- **Thirteen shell LLCs.** The SEC order's number. Named, incorporated in different
  states, each filing a partial 13F so no single filing revealed the whole.
- **1997 to 2019** — twenty-two years of undetected misfiling.
- **$4 million and $1 million.** For twenty-two years of concealing $32 billion. The
  penalty is the tell: the SEC's jurisdiction was total and its interest was
  procedural.
- **REG-112756-09.** A proposed regulation from 2009, still pending. A single
  unfinalized rule is the difference between the IRS being able to audit a church and
  not. Somewhere there is a docket with a comment period that never closed.
- The **990-T** as the only public window. The Church's most detailed public financial
  document is a return describing exactly the parts of itself it insists are not
  religious.
- The reconstruction attack: you cannot see the church, but you can see every
  corporation the church owns, because corporations have to tell their Secretary of
  State who owns them. **The dark zone has a shape, and the shape is visible from
  underneath.**
