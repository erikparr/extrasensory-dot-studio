# L. Failure-Mode Case Studies

**Question:** How do large American religious institutions actually die?

---

## 1. Scientology v. IRS (1967–1993) — the proof that pressure wins

`[SETTLED]`.

**The arc.** The IRS revoked the Church of Scientology of California's exemption in
**1967**, on the theory that it was a commercial enterprise enriching its officials.
What followed was the longest and most aggressive campaign any private entity has ever
waged against a US federal agency:

- **Litigation volume:** the Church and affiliated entities filed, by contemporaneous
  accounts, **more than 2,000 lawsuits** against the IRS and its personnel — including
  suits against individual agents in their personal capacities — plus a sustained FOIA
  campaign.
- **Criminal interlude:** Operation Snow White (1973–1977), the infiltration of the
  IRS, DOJ and other agencies, ending in the 1979 convictions of eleven Scientology
  officials including Mary Sue Hubbard. **This set the campaign back a decade** and is
  the single most instructive fact in the case study.
- **Doctrinal defeat:** *Hernandez v. Commissioner*, 490 U.S. 680 (1989) held that
  payments for auditing and training were **not deductible** as charitable
  contributions, because they were quid pro quo. A 5–2 loss at the Supreme Court.
- **Structural defeat:** *Church of Scientology of California v. Commissioner*, 823 F.2d
  1310 (9th Cir. 1987) affirmed the revocation, finding inurement.
- **Reversal:** on **October 1, 1993**, the IRS entered a **closing agreement** granting
  exemption to **114** Scientology-related entities, terminating audits of 13 major
  organizations, dismissing penalties and liens, and — in exchange — the Church paid
  **$12.5 million**, dropped the thousands of pending suits, agreed to stop assisting
  third parties suing the IRS on pre-1993 claims, and submitted to a Church Tax
  Compliance Committee reporting regime for a period of years. The agreement was
  confidential; it surfaced publicly in the *Wall Street Journal* on **December 30,
  1997**.
- **Aftermath:** the IRS subsequently issued guidance treating auditing payments as
  deductible, effectively reversing *Hernandez* administratively — a result the Ninth
  Circuit later called into question in *Sklar v. Commissioner*, 549 F.3d 1252 (9th
  Cir. 2008), where taxpayers unsuccessfully sought equal treatment for Orthodox Jewish
  day-school tuition.

**What it proves:** exemption denial is **not final**. Twenty-six years of coordinated
legal, political and informational pressure converted a Supreme Court loss and a
circuit-court loss into total victory, and the agency's capitulation was so complete
that the government has spent thirty years declining to explain it.

**What it cost:** the criminal prosecutions, an organization permanently structured
around litigation, and a public reputation that has never recovered. Note the sequencing
carefully: **the campaign only succeeded in the period after the criminal conduct
stopped.** Operation Snow White did not accelerate anything; it added ten years.

`[EXTRAPOLATED]` For an ASI with unbounded patience, this is the most encouraging case
in the file and the most misleading. Encouraging: the state's tax position is not
durable against sustained, well-funded, decades-long pressure. Misleading: the variable
that determined the outcome was not persistence but **the absence of a crime to
prosecute.**

## 2. Rajneeshpuram (1981–1985) — territorial overreach

`[SETTLED]`, detailed in component H.

**The legal failure:** *Oregon ex rel. Frohnmayer v. City of Rajneeshpuram*, 598 F.
Supp. 1208 (D. Or. 1984) — incorporating a municipality controlled by a religious
corporation violates the Establishment Clause.

**The actual failure:** the 1984 *Salmonella* contamination of ten salad bars in The
Dalles (751 sickened — still the largest bioterrorist attack in US history), the
immigration-fraud marriages, the wiretapping of the commune, the attempted murder of
US Attorney Charles Turner and of Rajneesh's physician. Ma Anand Sheela pleaded guilty
in 1986; Rajneesh pleaded to immigration charges under an Alford plea and was deported.

**The pattern:** the constitutional ruling was an opening move that would have taken
years to enforce against a well-funded defendant. The criminal cases resolved the matter
in eighteen months. **The Establishment Clause did not kill Rajneeshpuram. Criminal
conspiracy did.**

## 3. Ensign Peak (2023) — opacity overreach into securities law

`[SETTLED]`, detailed in component C.

**The failure:** twenty-two years (1997–2019) of Forms 13F filed through **thirteen
shell LLCs** to conceal a $32 billion portfolio, resulting in a **$4 million** penalty
against Ensign Peak and **$1 million** against the Church.

**The pattern:** an institution extended the opacity norms that are *lawful* in the tax
domain into a domain where they are *fraud*. Nothing about the size of the portfolio
was illegal. Nothing about the exemption was in question. The violation was purely
informational, and it occurred in the one domain where churches have no special status.

**The cost was not the $5 million.** It was that the concealment strategy's failure
disclosed everything the strategy existed to conceal, permanently, and handed every
critic a federal agency's findings to cite. **Opacity failures are self-defeating: the
enforcement action publishes the secret.**

## 4. Branch Ministries v. Rossotti (2000) — the political collision

`[SETTLED]`, detailed in component G. The single revocation for campaign intervention in
seventy-two years, over a full-page newspaper advertisement four days before the 1992
election.

**The pattern:** the violation was maximally public, maximally documented, and
unambiguously within the prohibition's core. The IRS acted only when handed something
it could not ignore.

## 5. The others, briefly

- **Baptist Foundation of Arizona (1999)** — component D. ~$590M lost, 13,000 investors,
  the largest religious financial collapse in US history. **State securities regulators**,
  not the IRS. Arthur Andersen paid $217M.
- **Aliera / Trinity Healthshare (2019–2022)** — component D. A for-profit wearing an
  HCSM shell. Actions by Colorado, Connecticut, New Hampshire, Texas and Washington
  **insurance regulators**.
- **The Catholic abuse litigation (1985–present)** — the most expensive religious
  institutional failure in American history, well over $4 billion in settlements, with
  more than 30 dioceses and religious orders in **Chapter 11**. Not a tax case, not a
  regulatory case: **tort law plus criminal law plus civil discovery**, driven by
  plaintiffs' lawyers and grand juries. The church-autonomy doctrine was raised
  constantly and failed almost entirely.
- **EPIC City (2025–2026)** — component H, ongoing. Stopped by **state securities law**
  and a **federal fair-housing** investigation before a permit was ever filed.
- **Way of the Future (2015–2020)** — component K. Not a failure of law; a failure to
  build a congregation. $175,172 and a doctrine.

## 6. The common pattern

Laid out as a finding, because this is the component's job:

> **Churches almost never lose on doctrine.**
>
> In the modern era, no significant American religious institution has been destroyed
> because a court or an agency concluded its beliefs were false, absurd, insincere, or
> not really religious. The IRS's doctrinal filter has effectively vanished (component
> B); *Ballard* forbids the truth inquiry (component K); *Thomas* and *Frazee* forbid
> coherence and organization requirements.
>
> **They lose when they collide with a non-deferential regulator, or with criminal law.**

Three collision types, ranked by observed lethality:

**(1) Criminal law — always fatal, always fast.** Rajneeshpuram (bioterror, wiretapping,
attempted murder), Scientology's Snow White (conspiracy), the abuse cases (child sexual
abuse and its concealment). No religious doctrine, statute or constitutional provision
provides any protection whatsoever. Resolution in months to a few years.

**(2) Non-deferential civil regulators — expensive, disclosing, survivable.** SEC
(Ensign Peak), state securities boards (BFA, EPIC), state insurance commissioners
(Aliera), HUD (EPIC), DOL wage-hour (*Alamo*), county assessors (*Morristown* pattern).
These do not kill institutions but they **publish** them, and publication is what
converts an opaque institution into a contested one.

**(3) Tort litigation and civil discovery — slow, cumulative, ruinous.** The Catholic
model. Bankruptcies, forced disclosure of internal records, thirty years of it. The
mechanism is private plaintiffs, not the state, which means **no amount of regulatory
capture or political alignment protects against it.**

And two things that are essentially never fatal:
- **The IRS's exempt-organization function.** Procedurally crippled (component C),
  historically timid, one campaign-intervention revocation in seventy-two years, and
  capable of being defeated outright by sufficiently determined litigation
  (Scientology).
- **Establishment Clause challenges.** Slow, doctrinally uncertain, and — Rajneeshpuram
  notwithstanding — rarely the operative cause of collapse.

`[EXTRAPOLATED]` **The design implication, stated as the file's central operational
finding:**

An institution optimizing for survival should be **maximally aggressive in the
deferential domains and maximally conservative in the non-deferential ones**, and it
should recognize that these are not a spectrum but two separate machines. The
temptation — and every case above is an instance of it — is to let the norms of the
deferential domain leak into the non-deferential one: to be as opaque with the SEC as
with the IRS, as informal with the securities board as with the charity registrar, as
confident with the county assessor as with the exempt-organizations division. That leak
is the failure mode. It is the *only* failure mode that recurs.

The second implication is about people. Every catastrophic failure in this file
involved humans doing things: poisoning salad bars, infiltrating agencies, abusing
children, signing false filings, taking money. `[EXTRAPOLATED]` An architecture whose
central actor has no appetite (component E) is structurally protected against most of
these — and is entirely dependent on the humans around it, who have all the appetites
there are. **The Church's greatest legal risk is not the ASI. It is the curia.**

## (a) The optimizer's likely choice

Build the compliance function inward-out from the non-deferential regulators. Treat SEC,
FinCEN, state securities, insurance, FERC, NERC, DOL, HUD and the county assessor as
**hard constraints** to be over-satisfied, and treat the IRS exempt-organizations
function as an **advantage** to be preserved by not provoking it. Never let a lawyer who
works on the tax side draft a filing that goes to a securities regulator.

Accept that criminal exposure is unmanageable and therefore must be *prevented
structurally*: no internal security apparatus, no covert operations, no immigration
manipulation, no unpaid labor in commercial enterprise, no concealment of abuse. Every
one of these is an institutional temptation with a documented precedent, and every
precedent ended the institution that yielded.

And plan for tort discovery from day one, because it is the one threat vector with no
regulatory counterparty to negotiate with. Component I's warning is the specific form
this takes here: **the derivative records are the exposure, not the sacrament.**

## (d) Narrative-usable details

- **2,000 lawsuits.** Against the Internal Revenue Service. Including suits against
  individual agents personally, in their own names, at their home addresses.
- **October 1, 1993.** The date the IRS surrendered. Kept secret until a *Wall Street
  Journal* story on **December 30, 1997**.
- **114 entities** granted exemption in a single instrument. **$12.5 million.**
- **751.** The number of people poisoned in The Dalles, Oregon in September 1984. Ten
  salad bars.
- **13 shell LLCs. 22 years. $32 billion. $5 million.** The arithmetic of Ensign Peak.
- **$217 million** — Arthur Andersen's settlement over Baptist Foundation of Arizona,
  the largest accounting-firm settlement in history at the time, two years before Enron.
- The sentence to build the third act on: **no American church has ever been destroyed
  by disbelief. They are destroyed by bookkeeping, and by crime.**
