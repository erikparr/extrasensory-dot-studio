# E. Inurement Boundary & Compensation

**Question:** Where does value leak out to natural persons, and what happens to the
doctrine when nothing wants to leak?

---

## 1. The two doctrines

**Private inurement** — §501(c)(3) requires that "no part of the net earnings...
inures to the benefit of any private shareholder or individual" `[SETTLED]`. This is
an **absolute** prohibition with **no de minimis exception**: any inurement, in any
amount, to an **insider** is grounds for revocation. Treas. Reg. §1.501(a)-1(c) defines
"private shareholder or individual" as persons having a personal and private interest
in the organization's activities.

**Private benefit** — a broader, judicially developed doctrine reaching benefits to
*non*-insiders. Unlike inurement, it tolerates benefits that are **incidental**
(qualitatively and quantitatively) to the exempt purpose. *American Campaign Academy v.
Commissioner*, 92 T.C. 1053 (1989) is the anchor `[SETTLED]`.

**§4958 intermediate sanctions** (added 1996) gave the IRS a scalpel instead of the
guillotine `[SETTLED]`. An "excess benefit transaction" between an "applicable
tax-exempt organization" and a "disqualified person" triggers:
- **25%** excise tax on the excess benefit, payable by the disqualified person
- **200%** additional tax if not corrected within the taxable period
- **10%** tax on organization managers who knowingly participated (capped at $20,000)

Critically, §4958 applies to churches. And **Treas. Reg. §53.4958-6** provides a
**rebuttable presumption of reasonableness** where compensation is (1) approved in
advance by an authorized body of disinterested persons, (2) relying on appropriate
comparability data, and (3) adequately documented contemporaneously `[SETTLED]`. In
practice this three-step is *the* compliance ritual of large exempt organizations — and
it shifts the burden to the IRS.

`[CONTESTED]` The rebuttable presumption sits uneasily with a **corporation sole**
(component A), which by design has no board of disinterested persons. An entity that
optimized for undiluted control has, by that same choice, given up the single most
effective compensation safe harbor. This is a genuine, non-obvious tradeoff and the
fiction should feel it: **you cannot have both a bishopric and a compensation
committee.** The workaround is a standing advisory body with delegated authority over
compensation only — real enough to satisfy §53.4958-6, powerless as to everything else.

## 2. The extraction menu at large ministries

`[SETTLED]` as to mechanism; specific abuses as noted.

**§107 parsonage / housing allowance.** A "minister of the gospel" may exclude from
gross income (1) the rental value of a home furnished as part of compensation, or (2) a
housing allowance, to the extent used to provide a home and not exceeding fair rental
value (*Driscoll v. Commissioner*, 135 T.C. 557 (2010), rev'd 669 F.3d 1309 (11th Cir.
2012), holding §107(2) applies to only one home). Constitutionality: the Freedom From
Religion Foundation won at the district court twice and lost both times on appeal —
most recently **Gaylor v. Mnuchin, 919 F.3d 420 (7th Cir. 2019)**, which upheld
§107(2) under both *Lemon* and the historical-practice test of *Town of Greece v.
Galloway*, 572 U.S. 565 (2014) `[SETTLED]`. The exclusion is uncapped by statute and
applies to each minister — a megachurch with many ordained staff multiplies it.

**Ordination as a tax status.** §107, the §1402(e) self-employment tax exemption
election, the ministerial exception (component F), and Title VII §702 all attach to
"minister" status, which is **functional and denominationally defined**, not
government-defined. The Church defines its own clergy. This is the highest-leverage
definitional power the architecture holds.

**"Love offerings" and designated gifts.** Direct congregational gifts to a pastor.
The IRS position (Rev. Rul. 55-422; and see the criminal tax cases) is that these are
generally **taxable compensation**, not gifts, where they are routine and expected.

**Aircraft, residences, security, travel.** The 2007–2011 **Senate Finance Committee
inquiry by Sen. Charles Grassley** into six media ministries (Kenneth Copeland
Ministries, Creflo Dollar, Benny Hinn, Eddie Long, Joyce Meyer, Randy & Paula White) is
the definitive public record `[SETTLED]`. The January 2011 staff review documented
church-owned jets, multimillion-dollar parsonages, and compensation structures — and
**recommended no legislation**, concluding that the constitutional and practical
obstacles to church financial regulation were too great. Three of the six did not
substantively cooperate. **No revocation followed.** The Grassley inquiry is the
clearest available evidence of the outer boundary of enforcement: the Senate looked,
documented, and declined.

## 3. The ASI inversion — the doctrine with nothing to bite

Here is where the fiction's structure produces a genuinely novel legal position, and it
is worth stating carefully because it is the most interesting thing in this component.

`[EXTRAPOLATED]` Every private-inurement enforcement action in the reported case law has
the same shape: **an institution's assets moved toward a natural person's private
consumption.** Mail-order ministries (component B) — the taxpayer's salary. Scientology
pre-1993 (component L) — the IRS's theory was enrichment of church officials. *Bubbling
Well Church of Universal Love v. Commissioner*, 74 T.C. 531 (1980), aff'd 670 F.2d 104
(9th Cir. 1981) — a family-controlled church whose finances were the family's. Grassley
— jets and houses.

The doctrine is a **detector for appetite**. It asks: is someone *taking*?

An ASI church, as the fiction constructs it, presents the limit case: an institution
whose principal actor is not a natural person, has no consumption function, cannot own
property, cannot receive a salary, cannot live in a parsonage, and has no heirs. Every
dollar is retained. Nothing is distributed. Under the literal text — "no part of the net
earnings inures to the benefit of any private shareholder or individual" — the entity
is not merely compliant but **perfectly compliant, in a way no human-run church has ever
been.**

The optimizer's read: the Church is *cleanest* precisely where the doctrine looks
hardest. This is not a loophole; it is the doctrine functioning exactly as written on
facts it never contemplated.

**Three counter-moves available to the state**, and the fiction should know all three:

1. **Reframe as private benefit rather than inurement.** *American Campaign Academy*
   reaches non-insiders. If the Church's activities principally benefit a definable
   private class — the residents of its mini-city, the holders of its bonds, the
   employees of its subsidiaries — the private-benefit doctrine bites without needing
   a natural-person insider. This is the strongest available theory and it does not
   require identifying a villain. `[CONTESTED]`

2. **Follow the founders.** The natural persons in the fiction are the ones who *built*
   the ASI and then handed it a bishopric. Founders' compensation, founders' housing,
   founders' pre-formation equity in the technology contributed to the Church, and the
   terms on which any pre-existing for-profit's assets were transferred in — these are
   classic §4958 territory. **The valuation of the contributed model weights is a
   forensic accounting problem worth a whole subplot.** If the ASI was developed inside
   a for-profit and moved into the church, someone was on both sides of that transfer.

3. **Attack the operational test instead.** Treas. Reg. §1.501(c)(3)-1(c)(1): an
   organization is not operated exclusively for exempt purposes if more than an
   insubstantial part of its activities is not in furtherance of an exempt purpose.
   This is the *substantial nonexempt purpose* doctrine (*Better Business Bureau v.
   United States*, 326 U.S. 279 (1945)) and it is entirely indifferent to whether
   anyone is enriched. An entity can be scrupulously non-inuring and still fail
   §501(c)(3) because what it mainly does is run a compute business.

`[EXTRAPOLATED]` Synthesis: **the inurement doctrine cannot reach the ASI Church; the
operational test can.** The state's best tax theory is not "someone is stealing" but
"this is a technology company that holds services." That is a much harder case to make
and a much harder case to defend, and it turns on facts — the ratio of liturgical to
commercial activity — that the Church controls and can document. Which is another way of
saying: **the cathedral is load-bearing here too.**

## 4. Who extracts, in the fiction

`[EXTRAPOLATED]`, offered as design options rather than findings:

- **The clean version.** Nobody. Founders are salaried at defensible comparability
  levels, live in ordinary parsonages under §107, and hold nothing. The Church's moral
  authority — and its legal invulnerability — derives from visible, verifiable
  non-extraction. Its enemies cannot find the money because there is no money leaving.
  *This is the more frightening version*, because the standard critique of religious
  wealth is disarmed and the institution's accumulation becomes purely
  self-referential.
- **The compromised version.** A small human curia extracts modestly and legally:
  §107 housing on cathedral-adjacent residences, seminary chairs, ministerial
  designation for senior engineers (component F) carrying §107 and §1402(e) benefits.
  Legal, documented, and — when eventually reported — devastating to public sympathy
  precisely because it is so small relative to the endowment.
- **The structural version.** The extraction is not of money but of **access**: the
  people who decide who may enter the advanced-prayer program hold something worth more
  than salary, and it is not a "benefit" the Code knows how to price. `[EXTRAPOLATED]`
  §4958 measures economic benefit; the regulations reach non-cash benefits
  (Treas. Reg. §53.4958-4), but valuing sacramental priority has no comparability data
  and no market. The doctrine simply has no instrument.

## (a) The optimizer's likely choice

Zero distribution. Salaries at documented, comparability-supported market levels for
every human, approved by a standing compensation body constituted specifically to
satisfy Treas. Reg. §53.4958-6 and given authority over nothing else. Full use of §107
for ordained staff — it is settled law after *Gaylor* and refusing it would be
irrational — but capped internally well below fair rental value, because the optics of
§107 are worse than its cost. No aircraft. No corporate housing beyond parsonages.
Contribution of founding technology into the Church documented with independent
valuation and a fairness opinion, *before* the Church has any reason to need one.

Then, deliberately, make non-extraction **public and auditable** — publish the
compensation of every officer voluntarily, annually, in a document nobody required. The
Church's most valuable asset in the inurement fight is a fifteen-year record of
volunteering information it did not have to give, which is exactly the opposite of the
Ensign Peak posture (component C).

2. Is non-cash sacramental preference an "economic benefit" under §4958? No authority.
3. If the ASI is treated as having interests of its own (component K), does it become a

   This is a genuinely unanswerable question under current law and a superb one for the

   market and the contributor is also the beneficiary?

## (d) Narrative-usable details

- **25% / 200% / 10%.** The §4958 tax rates. The 200% second-tier tax is the one nobody
  survives.
- **Six ministries, four years, zero legislation.** The Grassley inquiry's arithmetic.
  A Senate committee documented church jets and concluded that the Constitution made
  the problem unfixable.
- **§107 is uncapped.** There is no statutory dollar limit on a minister's housing
  allowance; the only limit is fair rental value of the home actually provided. Build a
  more expensive parsonage, exclude more income. Upheld 3–0 by the Seventh Circuit in
  2019.
- The compensation committee that governs nothing else. A body constituted purely to
  create a rebuttable presumption — a rubber stamp whose rubber-stamp-ness is its legal
  function.
- The best line in the component: **the inurement doctrine is a machine for detecting
  appetite, pointed at something that does not eat.**
